Why I Built This

Schools talk about climate. They don't measure it.

Growing up in India, I've seen climate change firsthand — hotter summers, irregular monsoons, worsening air quality. Despite all the awareness campaigns, not a single school in my city follows a structured carbon-emission measurement or reduction plan.


Existing calculators are too technical, too expensive, or built for other countries entirely. So I built one myself — India-specific, student-friendly, and free to use.

"Climate learning must be experiential. Students understand better when they measure real data instead of only reading theory."



Schools rely on symbolic practices — like planting trees — without measuring whether they actually reduce emissions. This tool changes that with real numbers.

How It Works

The methodology

India-specific emission factors from the CEA and India GHG Program, with IPCC 2006 defaults where local data was unavailable.

01
Define Scope & Boundary

Set the school's operational boundary and classified all activities under Scope 1, 2, and 3 per the GHG Protocol.

02
Collect Real Data

Fuel and electricity bills, school records, and structured surveys on commuting, uniforms, and books gathered from school authorities and vendors.

03
Apply Emission Factors

India-specific factors from CEA and India GHG Program applied. IPCC 2006 defaults used where local data was unavailable.

04
Stratified Sampling

One class per school level surveyed, then extrapolated to the full student population for accuracy without burdening every class.

05
Calculate & Annualise

Activity data converted to CO₂e and scaled across 240 school days, with per-capita and scope-wise breakdowns produced.

06
Validate & Refine

A pilot test improved survey clarity. Results cross-checked against international reference studies for consistency.

Why it matters

For Schools
